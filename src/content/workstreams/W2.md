title: AFC pruning protocol
tagline: Under a fair protocol, does functional connectivity pick better filters than simple scores?
---

The reproductions left one open question: is the *idea* behind ASF-S sound, even if the paper's numbers do not reproduce? The idea is to choose filters by how they shape the output layer's Artificial Functional Connectivity (AFC).

To answer that fairly, we built a separate, carefully tested package, AFC-Prune. It compares twelve ways of choosing filters under identical conditions:
- the same budget
- the same rule for how many filters each layer keeps
- the same recovery step afterwards
- several independently trained models
- a guard that logs every time anything reads the test set

The answer so far is sobering. Our corrected PGI beats random choice, but it loses to simple scores such as Taylor importance and weight magnitude, consistently across seeds.

The next step is a go/no-go decision, written down before the results arrive. Does functional connectivity carry information that simple scores miss? If it does not, PGI becomes one option among several in our continual learning work, and our contribution shifts to how capacity is shared between tasks.
