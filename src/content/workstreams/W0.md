A neural network that learns one task after another usually forgets the earlier ones. This is called *catastrophic forgetting*. One family of fixes, called *parameter isolation*, gives every task its own small subnetwork inside a shared model and protects it while later tasks are learned. These methods normally decide which weights a task keeps by their size, keeping the largest and freeing the rest.

We asked whether size is the right signal. A weight can be large and unimportant, or small and essential. The pruning method Artificial Structure Function Search (ASF-S) proposes a different signal: keep the filters that matter most for how the network *functions*, measured through the functional connectivity of its output layer. If that signal is better, it could give each task a smaller, better-chosen subnetwork, and leave more room for the tasks that come later.

Between June and August 2026 we reviewed the continual learning and pruning literature and wrote our proposal. The proposal describes two approaches, which became workstreams W3 and W4:
1. **Hard freezing**, which keeps each task's most important units fixed.
2. **Metaplastic soft consolidation**, which instead slows down learning on important weights.

The proposal deck is on the [proposal page](/proposal/).

What we learned since then changed the plan. Before building a continual learning method on top of ASF-S, we needed to know whether ASF-S itself works as reported. That question became workstreams W1 and W2.
