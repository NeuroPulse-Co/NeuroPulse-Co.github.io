id: W1
title: Reproducing ASF-S
tagline: Can we get the paper's results, and what do we learn when we cannot?
---

Everything we planned rests on one claim from the ASF-S paper: you can remove about 70% of a small network's parameters, chosen by Principal Gradient Importance (PGI), retrain only the final layer, and lose very little accuracy. Before building on that claim, we tested it.

We approached it from four directions:

- **CIFAR-10, step by step.** We rebuilt the method from scratch on the paper's own setting (Conv2Net), and checked every step against a number from the paper before moving on. Every step through pruning matched. The final step did not: retraining only the last layer loses 10.5 accuracy points where the paper reports 3.78. Fine-tuning the whole network gets much closer, which suggests how the paper's number was produced.
- **CIFAR-100, from the authors' code.** We ported the original scripts to a harder dataset and audited them line by line. We found and fixed three bugs. Even after the fixes, the method loses 12 points at about 70% pruning, and its filter choice is close to random.
- **MNIST, the simplest case.** The paper's LeNet baseline reproduces exactly, but simple magnitude pruning beats every ASF-S variant we tried.
- **BERT, a different kind of model.** Pruning the feed-forward layers costs under a point whatever chooses the neurons, so this setting cannot tell the methods apart.

A failed reproduction is not the same as a wrong paper. Our study pages separate what we have ruled out (our own bugs, tuning, seed noise) from what we only suspect (unstated details, ambiguous equations). Before the detailed write-ups are published here, we will share them with the authors.
